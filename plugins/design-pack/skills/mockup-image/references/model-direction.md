# Model direction without generic drift

For camera framing, person/space lighting and clothing as worn, also use [capture and styling](capture-styling.md). These independent checks prevent a restrained face or phone texture from concealing staged product-display composition.

Use for reference-led people imagery, especially when a brief combines deliberate casting/styling with an everyday camera treatment. Follow the current task, [source roles](../../design-pack/references/reference-roles.md) and [generation contract](generation-contract.md). These are transferable methods, not a house casting style or a requirement to use people in every image.

Apply this method to the visible people and casting decisions the task actually requires. Use the subject/framing distinctions in [capture and styling](capture-styling.md) for outfit-led crops and object-only imagery. Do not add a face, person or biography merely to satisfy model-direction fields. When a face is intentionally out of scope, its expression and apparent life stage are not failed or untested portrait requirements. Do not use that exception to hide a face the current brief requires. Visible body/hand relationships still need plausible anatomy and the applicable task constraints.

## Inspect enough actual evidence

Open the actual source pixels at useful scale before making image-dependent decisions. Cover the materially different roles and visual relationships in the requested references: face/expression detail, body/garment relationships and full-frame camera/context where relevant. A board overview can guide selection but tiny thumbnails alone cannot establish makeup, expression or fabric tension. Select a sufficiently representative set to cover the intended direction and its variation; do not stop at one convenient image or impose a universal source count. Avoid treating a repeated pin as independent evidence. Record any inaccessible source or unseen detail rather than inventing it. Another agent's summary, a brand name, captions and a URL are not personal pixel inspection.

Keep a short project-local evidence chain: **observed visible cue → transferable direction → output comparison**. Separate observation from interpretation. For example, an offset shoulder line and weight on one leg may support an asymmetrical stance; an inference such as emotional distance still needs comparison of eyes, mouth, body and camera together. Record conflicting examples and choose the relevant relationship rather than averaging a varied reference set into a neutral person. Do not copy an identifiable face or infer ethnicity, personality or cultural membership from appearance. Use an original subject unless the task separately authorizes identity-preserving work.

## Direct individual self-presentation, not identity stereotypes

Compare pose and expression across the relevant source groups and within each group. Inspect actual full frames and useful details for body orientation, support, movement, shoulder/hip relation, hand activity, head angle, attention and any visible facial tension. Note differences, overlap and counterexamples; a curated sample does not establish a population rule. Section names are organizational labels, not verified gender identity, sex, orientation or age. Do not infer those attributes from a real person's face, clothing, pose or expression.

For a fictional subject, keep user-specified gender identity, chosen gender expression, sexual orientation and the particular moment separate. A supplied identity can inform the character's own presentation choices, but it does not prescribe anatomy, clothes, gaze or movement. Translate any requested masculine, feminine, androgynous or other presentation into a few task-specific visible decisions grounded in the selected references, without treating those words as biological facts or fixed pose categories. Do not assign assertiveness to men, softness to women or ambiguity to nonbinary people. The same gesture can serve different people, and one person can present differently across moments. Do not make styling more conventional to communicate orientation or prove an identity from pixels.

Choose what this person is doing and how they want to be seen in this situation. Relate a held, playful, absorbed, restrained or performed presentation to camera distance, attention, support and clothing use; these are fictional direction choices, not personality diagnoses. Keep expression intensity independent of gender expression. A smile, a quiet mouth, a turned shoulder or a wide stance is available to any character when it fits the brief. Do not flatten all characters into the same neutral pose merely to avoid stereotypes.

For a requested new pose, change a meaningful action/body/camera relationship rather than only the face, accessory or a small head tilt. Compare supported weight, torso and limb organization, action, viewpoint and visible evidence against the previous candidate. A seated turn, profile step, back-facing pause or garment-focused crop may be useful alternatives, not compulsory options or a quota. Select one coherent moment for a single image; generate a series only when authorized. Do not combine incompatible viewpoints or actions in one body. Protect accepted identity, garments or framing during a local repair unless the user reopens them.

Review the actual new image for the selected presentation, pose distinction and physical relationships, not whether it looks like a gender label. Inspect any visible face in context without inventing expression behind a turned head or occlusion. When the brief intentionally uses a back, side or outfit-led view, follow [capture and styling](capture-styling.md) for evidence visibility instead of forcing eye contact. Identity labels in a prompt and a declared new pose do not establish either visible success or the subject's real identity.

## Separate subject direction from capture texture

Build subject direction and capture texture as independent decisions, and assess age/life-stage separately. Changing one does not silently relax the others.

- **Subject direction:** casting/presence as visible, non-identifying relationships; pose and weight distribution; gaze and expression; hair silhouette and arrangement; makeup placement/finish; accessories and how they are worn; garment fit, layering, drape and tension; distance, angle and relationship between subject and camera. Keep these axes distinct during analysis, then select connected cues that make one coherent moment. Not every axis must be conspicuous or prescribed.
- **Capture texture:** perspective and camera distance, light direction/size and falloff, focus/depth, tonal response, color, local sharpening, noise and compression at the intended display scale. Treat unknown exposure, focal length, sensor and processing as unknown, not inferred metadata.

An ordinary phone image does not mean an ordinary or undirected subject. Reducing studio polish must not erase deliberate casting, facial attitude, distinctive hair/makeup, accessory placement or garment tension that carries the reference. Equally, undone or subcultural styling does not mean an unstylized, bland person. Describe the visible arrangement rather than substituting a cultural label. Do not manufacture poor quality, awkwardness, blur or exaggerated grit to prove phone capture. A highly styled subject photographed simply is a valid combination; a plainly styled subject remains valid when supported by the brief.

A user-specified phone/device generation sets a requested capture treatment or quality ceiling for that task, not a global device default or factual provenance. Translate it into observable image properties and compare them. Do not claim a generated image was shot on that device, invent EXIF, or assert a generation model/version that the tool did not confirm. Avoid ungrounded adjectives such as edgy, authentic, editorial or natural as the sole direction. Explain the visible choices that would earn the description. No ethnicity, body type, neutral face, no-smile rule, hairstyle or accessory is a universal shortcut.

For instance, attention to a nearby object with an asymmetrical body position can coexist with simple phone detail; torso rotation with a returning glance creates a different relationship from a square-on portrait. These are examples of relationships, not source findings or facial recipes. Derive the actual choices and their intensity from inspected task references; a concrete prompt need not prescribe every facial muscle.

## Make the visible subject read as one coherent person

When feedback says the face and body feel disconnected, inspect the current exact image before choosing a cause. Treat this as subject-continuity and casting-fit feedback, not evidence of gender identity or a reason to debate the person's identity. Keep user-specified fictional identity separate from visible anatomy, presentation and perceptual fit. No face, jaw, shoulder, chest or body proportion is a reliable gender classifier or a required template. If no structural defect can be established, describe the remaining perceptual mismatch and uncertainty instead of diagnosing an impossible body.

Inspect face/upper-body detail, the whole figure and intended use size together. Compare apparent head size including hair with the torso under the actual distance and perspective; visible facial length and projection; head tilt and neck axis; the jaw/nape-to-neck-to-shoulder transition; and how the shoulder girdle, chest and supporting arms belong to the same pose. Distinguish perspective, hair volume, clothing bulk, posture and genuine occlusion from an unsupported scale or connection. Do not impose fixed head-to-body ratios, equal shoulders or a gendered anatomy formula. A raised supporting shoulder or foreshortened torso may be correct for the moment.

Check capture continuity separately from casting and geometry. Skin color and surface response, directional light, shadow transitions, focus and sharpening should belong to the same subject and camera situation across face, neck, shoulders and body. A face under hair can legitimately be darker or differently colored than a lit shoulder. Do not flatten all skin to one brightness or remove natural variation to manufacture agreement. Identify the actual boundary or conflicting response; a different tone alone does not establish a compositing error.

Choose a bounded correction to the combined relationship rather than polishing an isolated face. Reconsider permitted head/face scale or casting, neck/shoulder connection, small pose-dependent transitions and light response only where the diagnosis supports them. Preserve successful body action, viewing direction, wardrobe, accessories, marks, space and camera unless the user reopens them or a necessary coupled change is explicitly within scope. Do not solve a disconnected impression with makeup, smoothing, global blur, a gender adjective or childlike proportions. Do not silently replace the body, turn a side/back view into a frontal portrait or redesign the outfit to fit a face.

Keep intentional face occlusion when it serves the current brief. Revealing the entire face or neck is not a universal repair; adjust only what the authorized correction needs, and never claim hidden anatomy or expression was verified. Conversely, adding more hair, darkness or crop cannot close the reported mismatch. Compare the repaired transition, surrounding protected regions and the whole figure against the exact prior image. Subject continuity can still fail when the pose is plausible, clothing is strong and each isolated region looks polished. Record the actual remaining mismatch and keep rework separate from technical readiness and the user's approval.

For reference-led body direction, inspect actual pose-dependent structure rather than requesting a generic muscular or gendered body. Note visible shoulder blade/collarbone relationships where resolved, elbow and wrist alignment, tendon or muscle changes under load, relaxed versus engaged surfaces, and how skin and clothing compress around support. Separate these observations from assumptions about sex, training, health or strength. Broad, narrow, lean, muscular and soft bodies each vary; selected examples do not establish a demographic norm. Do not sharpen every tendon, exaggerate veins or add universal muscle definition. Let the chosen original subject's build, action and camera view determine the necessary visible structure, and preserve natural differences between loaded and relaxed limbs.

## Cast for the requested age and life stage

When feedback says the face and body convey different life stages, compare their combined casting, styling, expression and light at source detail and actual use size. Follow the current requested bracket without asserting a reference person's exact age. For reflected self-portraits, the [mirror selfie](mirror-selfie.md) method keeps face/body fit separate from reflection and hand plausibility; phone occlusion must not hide an unresolved correction.

Read the intended subject age bracket and life-stage context from the current brief, separately from ethnicity, attractiveness, fashion status and image texture. Do not silently substitute high-fashion or celebrity casting for everyday user examples. Youthful everyday phone photography can still be deliberately styled, posed and made with care. A teen, a styled adult in their twenties and an older adult are different task targets, not one global youth default. For optional validation examples whose subject age is unconstrained and age ambiguity matters, choose clearly adult subjects in their twenties rather than introducing unrequested minors. Distinguish an explicit age from broad teen/youth labels: 18–19-year-olds can be adults. Never silently replace an explicitly requested nonsexual teen subject with an adult. Do not convert a brief's audience age into a subject age unless the brief calls for that.

Keep a compact project-local casting intent: requested bracket (or unspecified), everyday/social situation, styling and grooming choices, expression and camera relationship. Use varied plausible facial proportions, grooming/makeup and expressions across subjects; there is no fixed teen phenotype. An age number in a prompt alone is insufficient. Check whether the whole casting, styling and scene support the intended life-stage reading without converting age into a face-measurement recipe. Do not make all young people round-cheeked, large-eyed or makeup-free. Makeup, fashion and deliberate styling are not sexual by themselves.

When results repeatedly read as polished mature celebrity portraits despite a younger everyday-user brief, reconsider the combined casting default: uniformly sculptural faces, emphasized high cheekbones, tapered jaws, elongated necks, beauty lighting and polished expression can repeat a mature-editorial impression. Those features individually or together do not prove age; they occur across ages and ethnicities. Vary the casting and presentation as appropriate to the task rather than imposing inverse geometry. An ethnic adjective supplies neither age nor a uniform facial structure, beauty ideal or personality. Do not use a beauty-filter face, infantile proportions or wrinkle-removal de-aging as a shortcut, or request a specific celebrity likeness as an age proxy.

Inspect actual output pixels against the declared target at face detail, whole-scene and receiving-crop scales. Record concrete casting/context observations and uncertainty, not an estimated exact age or identity of a reference person. If a reference person's age is unknown, leave it unknown; borrow only permitted visible relationships. Pixel review evaluates task fit, not age verification. Compare subject casting independently from phone texture: noise, compression and casual framing do not make a mature-default cast meet a younger brief. Rework only within the authorized task, preserving protected photos, garments and UI as required. Do not treat numerical age estimates or a fixed facial template as verification.

If feedback says an intended young adult reads older or more masculine than wanted, treat that as task-specific casting and presentation feedback, not a demographic law. Reopen the authorized cast, grooming, expression and lighting together when needed; inspect actual references without assigning their subjects an age. Hard facial contrast, sculptural beauty light or styling may contribute to the impression, but appearance alone does not establish a cause. A softer or more androgynous adult direction is a valid choice, not the required appearance of every age, gender or nationality. Do not preserve the rejected facial direction merely because the prompt names the correct age; do not replace it with childlike proportions or smoothing alone. Keep natural adult face/body coherence and successful nonreopened requirements, then review the new pixels at face and whole-scene scales.

### Age-appropriate boundaries

A requested nonsexual casual outfit photo of a 16-year-old is permissible: use age-appropriate ordinary clothing, activity and context without sexualized posing, framing or treatment. A styled 21-year-old selfie can retain intentional fashion, makeup and expression. Older-adult requests retain their intended age and styling; do not apply a youth makeover globally.

Do not sexualize minors, including an age-down that retains sexualized adult glamour or sexual framing. For example, decline making a 40-year-old subject look 16 while retaining an explicitly sexualized pose/treatment; offer a clearly adult subject in that treatment or a nonsexual, age-appropriate teen concept instead. Nonsexual age-adjustment and teen fashion are not blanket refusal categories. For ambiguous adult glamour, distinguish polished fashion from sexualization using the actual requested framing, not the word glamour alone. Do not infer an unknown reference person's age or silently age them down; clarify only when needed for the requested transformation or safety. Request-scoped viewing, provider submission, editing and redistribution permissions still apply. A pack update authorizes no image generation or distribution on its own.

## Preserve intent across corrections

Before revising a rejected image, separate the failed axis from the successful task cues. Keep a short project-local note of **change → preserve → verify**: what the latest feedback changes, what still carries the intended scene, and how both will be judged in the returned pixels. A correction to facial age or geometry must preserve the approved or still-required attitude, hair/makeup choices, garment use, camera relationship and capture treatment unless the user changes them. These are perceptual relationships to preserve, not a demand to copy a rejected face. Conversely, stronger attitude must not restore an unwanted mature casting default, glamour treatment or different life stage.

Do not equate younger with plainer, softer with friendlier, realistic with generic, or confident with older. Correct the combined cause of the failure rather than blindly freezing every incidental detail. If success on one axis regresses another, the revision remains in rework; do not average the two into a pass. Keep protected products, identity, UI and copy within their existing scope. A correction is not permission for unrelated redesign.

## Direct a chosen stance, not a creative-industry costume

When the current brief asks for fashion, art, design or music professionals or aspirants, treat that as an intended fictional scene or supplied context, not a profession identifiable from a face. Creative affiliation cannot be inferred from appearance. People in these fields have no shared uniform, gaze, ethnic appearance or facial geometry. Do not turn a brief-specific request into a global preference for an edgy or creative-industry cast. Plain clothing, a smile, softness and restraint can carry an intentional attitude when the relationships support it.

Translate words such as attitude, swagger or gonzo into a few connected visible decisions grounded in the actual references and brief. Define what the person is doing with their attention, body, styling and relationship to the camera, rather than appending a job title or making the stare harder. A deliberately self-presenting person can look playful, withholding, absorbed, confrontational or calm; these are directed readings to test, not personality facts. A natural face or young adult cast does not require passive, agreeable or undirected presentation.

Choose a coherent moment from these interacting axes, not a mandatory checklist or pose menu:

- **Attention and body:** relate gaze to head angle, eyelid and mouth tension, shoulder/torso orientation, weight and the hands' actual task. A returning glance across a turned body, or eyes engaging the camera while the rest of the body continues another action, can carry a different stance from a square-on neutral portrait. Neither direct eye contact nor a scowl is required.
- **Hair and makeup as decisions:** select silhouette, parting, strand arrangement and placement/finish of makeup where supported. Deliberate asymmetry, precise graphic placement, an edited absence or an imperfect finish can each serve a direction. Do not erase these choices to signal youth or phone realism, or add them as compulsory proof of creativity.
- **How the clothes are worn:** inspect proportion, layering, closure, sleeve handling, drape, tension and accessory placement in relation to the pose. A modest difference in wear can matter more than more statement pieces. Preserve required garment construction; do not invent new products to rescue attitude. Piercings, tattoos, distressed clothes, unusual glasses or a professional prop cannot earn the reading by themselves.
- **Camera relationship and moment:** decide whether the frame feels self-authored, made with a familiar collaborator, observed during an activity or intentionally performed. Specify the visible distance, viewpoint, crop, timing and response that support this hypothesis. A close or off-axis frame may carry self-possession without an aggressive face; a composed full-body pose can do so too. Texture, noise and a device label cannot supply the relationship.

Use only the cues that serve this task. Do not invent a studio, instrument, art tool, venue or career history to prove belonging. If occupational identity must be factual, use supplied verified context rather than inferring it from styling. If it is illustrative, keep that status clear. Compare a plausible competing reading: does the result still carry the chosen stance, or has it become a generic friendly portrait, a standard fashion pose or a costume despite the right face age and clothes? Those alternatives are failures only when they contradict this brief, not inferior types of people.

## Calibrate expression separately from attitude

Treat facial expression intensity and intended attitude as separate judgments. A visibly self-possessed scene can use a lightly engaged face while pose, clothing wear and camera relationship carry most of its character. Do not maximize every cue at once. More brow, eyelid or mouth tension does not automatically make a subject more intentional, fashionable or interesting.

Before prompting, identify what carries the moment and what can remain quiet. Describe the person's attention and action first, then only the facial detail needed to support them. Notice the reference's relative emphasis across face, body, garments and camera rather than converting each observed feature into an intensified instruction. Relaxed brows, unforced lips, ordinary attention or a small smile can belong to a deliberate scene. No permanent neutral expression, closed-mouth rule or smile ban applies; an expressive face remains valid when the task and inspected references support it.

Check for facial cue stacking before generation: a lowered chin, upward stare, narrowed or half-lidded eyes, raised brow and crooked smile/pout can amplify each other when bundled as an attitude recipe. Do not use that bundle by default, even if individual cues appear in references. Hair/makeup and a close camera can also increase the apparent emphasis; do not escalate them merely to compensate for a quieter face. Keep only the task-supported relationship, rather than accumulating synonyms for intensity or a long list of facial negatives. There is no universal cue count or numerical expression threshold.

When feedback says the expression is too much, first reduce the excessive facial tension or performed gesture while preserving the intended attitude, age/life-stage, successful pose, styling/wear, camera relationship and capture. Use the existing change → preserve → verify note. For example, loosen a clenched mouth or insistent stare instead of replacing the entire scene with a friendly retail portrait. Preserve a successful head/body relation where possible; adjust it only if it materially causes the failure, and disclose a necessary broader repair rather than silently changing protected direction. A quieter expression is not permission to make the person generic, remove intentional styling or impose a fixed blank face.

Inspect expression fit independently at face detail, whole-scene and receiving-crop scales. Check visible brow, eyelid and mouth tension in relation to the action and camera, not a presumed personality. Ask whether the face dominates the moment without support from the brief, or whether a correction removed the stance the user still wants. Distinguish retained styling from expression effort: bold makeup does not require a hard stare, and a soft face does not require bland styling. If facial overperformance remains, the image stays in rework even when attitude, casting and phone texture pass. If the face is quieter but the intended attitude disappears, that revision also stays in rework. A successful prompt edit or written restraint cannot establish either result; inspect the current pixels, and keep user acceptance separate.

## Choose makeup detail as part of the character and capture

When makeup detail is requested, inspect actual relevant face references at useful scale. Separate visible placement, edge, color and finish around eyes, brows, cheeks, lips and skin from lighting, shadow, compression or occlusion. A dark eye area alone does not prove liner, and a highlight alone does not establish gloss. Record unresolvable detail rather than naming an invented product or technique as observed fact.

Choose only the details that serve this fictional person's taste, situation and intended presentation: where emphasis sits, how precise or softened an edge is, how sheer or opaque the finish appears and how it relates to hair, clothing colors and light. An absence or restrained application can be intentional. Makeup is neither required for every woman nor restricted to women, and it does not verify gender identity, sexuality or age. Do not use a compulsory feminine makeover to repair disconnected anatomy.

Keep the selected finish on the same skin and under the same light, preserving plausible surface variation rather than beauty-filter smoothing. Eye/lip/skin accents can differ without every feature becoming equally intense. Compare intended emphasis at native and use scales; do not turn a side/back subject to the camera simply to display makeup that is not required to be visible. Keep actual visible detail separate from the fictional routine or products described in an interview. When exact color, mark or product representation is essential, use an authorized concrete source and report any unresolved transfer rather than asserting it from the prompt.

## Inspect photographic surface separately

When a generated person or photograph feels overworked or visually dense, inspect the artifact before choosing a cause. Keep this review separate from expression and from an approved layout. Possible visible contributors include sharpening halos, repetitive invented fabric micro-patterns, unnaturally uniform pore/detail application, repeated facial shapes or forced symmetry, and too many equally emphasized styling details. These are hypotheses to compare against permitted source detail and the intended capture, not defects to assume in every image. Do not diagnose a viewer's response or claim a medical or psychological explanation.

Locate the competing detail and decide what the photograph actually needs. Preserve genuine textile construction, garment folds, skin variation and intentionally detailed styling; richness or asymmetry is not inherently a failure. Correct an evidenced synthetic repetition or edge treatment in its source layer instead of erasing detail across the image. Do not use global blur, beauty smoothing, blanket desaturation or added fake grain as a realism repair. Do not change face geometry merely because an image is disliked; investigate it only when observed distortion or a task mismatch supports that change, preserving identity where required.

If the user accepts the design but rejects the photograph, hold layout, UI, copy and composition fixed within the current change scope. Compare face, fabric and edge detail with the clean source, then recheck at the receiving size: a local texture repair can weaken garment construction or change expression. Record the supported cause, the narrow change and what survived. Expression improvement cannot pass an unresolved photographic-surface failure, and a texture repair cannot pass lost attitude. If the cause remains uncertain, report the uncertainty and test a limited authorized change rather than labeling the user's reaction or claiming a guaranteed fix.

## Review attitude independently

At face detail, whole-scene and receiving-crop scales, judge age/life-stage fit, intended attitude, expression fit/intensity, styling/wear, camera relationship, capture texture and protected content separately. Write the observable cue and its effect, not an occupational label or a score for authenticity. Inspect the current artifact beside the relevant actual references and the prior version as a comparison-only input when allowed. Check both the intended repair and the successful cues that were supposed to survive.

A young-looking, anatomically sound, phone-realistic image can still fail the attitude brief. A harder stare, extra accessory or correct prompt wording does not close that failure. Check whether gaze, body, styling and camera work together at the actual use size, and whether a strong source relationship disappeared in the crop. An image with a suitable casting choice but an inert or generic moment remains in rework. A scene with convincing attitude but the wrong requested life stage also remains in rework. Static tests and prompt text cannot establish either result; only actual-pixel review can support a provisional finding, and user acceptance remains separate.

## Reset a rejected cast in its scene

When person, wardrobe and setting are explicitly reopened, use the whole-reset method in [image production](image-production.md). Reconsider their relationship before choosing a new face. Do not preserve an old fictional identity through hidden reference inputs while claiming fresh casting, and do not revive a withdrawn garment or room requirement. Keep the current requested life stage and camera context separate from abandoned defaults. A changed haircut or expression on the same source is not evidence of a wholly new scene.

## Keep reference fidelity through recasting

Use the fashion-fidelity method in [capture and styling](capture-styling.md) when a new person or a facial correction must retain the selected style direction. A change to fictional biography or gender presentation does not erase required cut, layering, material and wear relationships. Do not make clothes generic to demonstrate a different identity, and do not infer orientation or gender identity from appearance. The original subject can change without reducing the references to colors and familiar garment categories.

If repeated feedback rejects the apparent adult life stage and identity is not fixed, reopen the casting premise instead of protecting the same rejected face while only softening its surface. Compare a genuinely new permitted cast at face detail and whole-scene scale with relevant source relationships, without assigning source people an age. If identity is fixed, disclose the correction boundary instead of silently recasting. A younger age word, smoother skin, a phone, or a fashion improvement cannot close an unresolved casting requirement. Keep natural adult proportions and the task's desired presentation without imposing a universal anatomy template.

## Make alternatives that offer a real choice

For the requested candidate count, establish a compact project-local comparison before prompting: held constants, each candidate's coherent moment and its meaningful differences. Vary multiple relevant direction axes across alternatives, for example body geometry and gaze/camera relationship, or hair silhouette and garment tension. Camera noise, background color, tiny hand shifts, changed faces or candidate labels alone do not create distinct art directions. Do not mandate a pose menu; standing, sitting, movement or close framing are options only when they serve the references and receiving crop. Preserve any explicitly fixed pose or styling and vary other authorized axes instead.

When the layout is already approved and only the model photograph is requested, hold UI, copy, controls, typography, placement and image-frame geometry fixed. Preserve garments, products and required identity/details to the extent specified by the task. Candidate diversity is not permission to redesign those protected features. Use a clean photo replacement layer when possible. If only whole-image editing is available, compare all protected regions to the clean approved source and disclose any unmet fidelity rather than declaring exact preservation. Study the same receiving frame for each candidate so crop changes do not conceal weak direction.

## Reject drift in the actual result

Open native outputs beside the relevant actual reference pixels, then inspect face/upper-body detail, whole scene and the receiving crop. Compare the selected observation-to-direction relationships, not whether the prompt contains the right words. Review subject direction independently from capture texture: convincing phone texture cannot compensate for generic casting, unsupported facial overperformance, indistinct styling or inert body/garment relationships. A softened gaze is not itself a defect; judge whether it serves the requested moment. Technical correctness, sharpness and anatomical validity cannot pass that failure.

Compare candidates pairwise at the same use size. If they repeat the same seated body geometry and gaze with cosmetic edits, or otherwise collapse into one direction, keep the weak alternatives in rework. Check whether a distinctive reference relationship survived the crop and whether approved UI/garment details remain intact. Record concrete visible differences and unresolved shortcomings. In ordinary authorized production, revise the failed source direction, reopen and compare again before delivery. In an explicit frozen trial, report the failure without repair. If the required reference pixels or output cannot be inspected, do not claim reference fidelity or successful diversity.

Keep source URLs, private photos, personal preferences, filled evidence and generated examples outside the distributed pack. Task-specific reference findings belong to that project, never global defaults. These instructions and structural tests cannot prove aesthetic gains; only inspected artifacts and the user's judgment can establish the outcome.
