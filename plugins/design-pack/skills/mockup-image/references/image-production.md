# Make and place the image

For reference-led photographic framing, lighting and outfits, use [capture and styling](capture-styling.md). Observe the camera/scene relationship before staging the subject, and review framing, lighting and styling separately from texture.

Start from the image's job and the actual receiving frame. Use the optional [image brief](../templates/image-brief.md) only for consequential decisions. No fixed studio, cinematic, candid, luxury or shallow-focus look applies.

Choose the visual subject and framing mode through [capture and styling](capture-styling.md): person, worn outfit, wearer-view detail or object may lead the image. A supporting object can become the requested primary subject, with its actual master/source role preserved. Do not expand its printed portrait or a reflected detail into an unrequested live model. Make the required evidence legible through composition rather than treating every image as a face-led scene. Separate views may be useful only when authorized; a pack-only improvement does not itself authorize new samples.

## Content truth and visual quality

Preserve the requested medium. A photograph cannot silently become illustration or SVG because the route is easier. Distinguish factual product evidence, authentic supplied content and authorized illustrative generation. Photorealistic invention does not establish material, fit, color, provenance or construction.

Read actual source pixels for the captured moment, camera/subject relationship, expression, pose, lighting, color and texture. Camera settings and unseen details remain inference. An end-user photograph may be highly intentional and beautifully made; posing, styling and crisp detail do not make it inauthentic. Do not manufacture bad light, awkward framing or blur to signify everyday use. Follow [source roles](../../design-pack/references/reference-roles.md), including constraint scope by medium. An interface palette alone does not authorize photographic desaturation or recoloring.

For factual items, use approved real assets and known specifications. Do not invent seams, fasteners, logos or geometry and pass them off as catalogue evidence. Missing required assets leave dependent production open; independent planning can continue.

For model imagery, distinguish reference-grounded casting, attitude and styling from capture texture using [model direction](model-direction.md). A phone-camera treatment is not permission to weaken the subject direction. Inspect unsupported surface repetition and over-sharpening separately from expression; do not repair realism with global blur or added grain. Evaluate expression intensity separately from intended attitude; a quieter face can retain deliberate pose, wear and camera relationships. An age correction must not make intentional styling or stance generic; an attitude correction must not age the subject or substitute a costume. Recheck each axis in the actual result.

## Choose a local repair or a whole new direction

Read the latest correction before preserving a previous master. A local repair keeps successful elements within its narrow change boundary. An explicit whole reset reopens the named axes and cancels their old preservation locks. Keep a short private note of reopened axes, still-current requirements and rejected comparison-only material. Do not discard unrelated current constraints merely because the task says to start over.

When the user reopens person, outfit and space together, design all three anew rather than changing a hairstyle, sleeve or wall on the old image. Do not carry the rejected identity photo, garment list, room description or flattened image forward as a hidden edit or continuity input. Check both the actual image arguments and inherited prompt text. Retain an old asset only for a part the current scope explicitly preserves; comparison viewing is not permission to condition the new generation on it. An original fictional cast must not silently reproduce a real reference person's identity.

Read the organized references through [source roles](../../design-pack/references/reference-roles.md), including current section coverage and conflicts. Construct one situated moment before committing the master: who is present as an illustrative subject, how the outfit is worn, what the body is doing or attending to, why the space and camera position belong to that moment, and how light reaches them. These are art-direction decisions, not invented facts about the user or a real reference person.

When the brief calls for an individual with intentional taste, develop a provisional fictional person rather than a fashion inventory. Choose only relevant reasons and routines: what they value or use, practical constraints, how they maintain or change their hair or nails, and how a bag, its plausible contents or a phone case fits the day's activity. A fictional occupation may inform these choices when useful, but occupation does not determine style; avoid a uniform of stereotypes. Mark invented biography as a design hypothesis, never a fact inferred about a real reference person. Use authorized project context privately, without importing private stories or people into the reusable method.

Connect the chosen details through visible consequences: grooming and clothing can have different upkeep, a carried item affects bag shape and weight, a case affects the actual phone grip, and an activity changes what is reachable or worn. Select the few details the image can support. Not every nail, bag item or personal motive must be exposed to the camera, and hidden contents should not be claimed as visible evidence. Reject decorative biography that does not improve the scene; do not add accessories, clutter, branded tokens or an itemized bag display just to illustrate every idea.

Let each choice constrain the others. Clothing volume, closure and material should respond to the chosen body position and light. Spatial scale, furniture or objects should relate to the body's support, movement and camera view. The camera or mirror arrangement should make that moment physically plausible and reveal the intended relationships. A lived space need not be cluttered, shabby or domestic; do not add generic props as proof of authenticity. A person can intentionally pose without becoming a catalogue mannequin.

Choose a few decisive relationships instead of independently polishing a face, selecting fashionable items and furnishing a backdrop. Compare the whole composition early enough to replace a weak premise. If the cast could be swapped into any generic showroom without changing the image's meaning, inspect what actually links person, wear, place and camera. This is a diagnostic, not a demand for a narrative prop or unusual venue.

For reset scope, inspect whether the new subject, outfit and space genuinely changed in the requested ways. Changed labels, hair color, accessories or wall color alone do not demonstrate a new overall direction. Do not infer biometric identity, occupation, exact age or cultural membership from that review. A required constant may legitimately survive; distinguish it from an abandoned default. Keep capture, expression, adult-life-stage, mirror geometry or other still-current requirements independently reviewable.

For fashion-led scenes, apply [capture and styling](capture-styling.md) to the particular cut, layering, material and wear relationships that distinguish the sources. Coherent person/space storytelling must not flatten those choices into conventional basics. When a local defect is repaired, recheck the retained fashion direction as well as the changed feature; a technically tidier garment can still be the wrong interpretation. Keep style fidelity and casting fit independent, and reopen any axis the latest feedback rejects.

## Make personal details explainable without turning them into a costume

When the task requests a fictional interview-ready person, make consequential tattoos and accessories specific enough to explain without improvising a new history after every question. Keep a short project-local account of the selected design or object, form/material/placement, how it was obtained or chosen within the fictional story, why that form matters and how it is worn or used now. A motif can be decorative, sentimental, practical, playful or simply preferred; do not force trauma, occupation or cultural membership as an explanation. Multiple objects need not share one symbolic theme. No tattoo, piercing, jewelry or accessory is compulsory, and variety does not mean adding more to every person.

Connect the story to a few observable decisions. A tattoo's line, scale, placement and relationship to moving skin, or an accessory's closure, wear, load and position can support the character's choices. Use original illustrative designs or authorized assets, not an identifiable person's tattoo as copied biography. If a consequential mark or insert must be shown precisely, apply the supporting-artifact method below: resolve and inspect its design at useful scale before relying on a tiny scene depiction. Do not claim a standalone master exists when only a written specification exists.

Separate fictional interview facts from source evidence and rendered evidence. Keep the chosen account consistent across revisions, but do not claim hidden marks, miniature lettering, provenance or physical manufacture are verified in the image. Note which details are visible, partly occluded or off-frame and whether that meets the brief. Do not expose every object, turn the subject frontally or invent extra props solely to illustrate the biography. Review pose-dependent placement, surface/light and any required design fidelity alongside the main character and clothing; an elaborate story cannot excuse an incoherent or unreadable required detail.

When a user rejects the relationship between a character's styling and personal details, reopen the fictional premise as well as the individual object. Compare the clothing's material/cut/wear, jewelry, marks and their stated reasons as choices made by this particular person over time. Distinguish an intentional contrast or accumulated taste from an unrelated collection of attributes. Do not force every possession to match one theme or infer that everyone who wears a style must have the same tattoos, but do not dismiss the user's requested coherence as mere diversity either.

Keep a concise, concrete account of why the consequential choices belong together or deliberately differ. A new outfit may coexist with an older mark when the task supports that history; if the latest feedback rejects the inherited mark or story, it is no longer automatically protected. Replace the weak design or premise within scope instead of inventing an elaborate after-the-fact explanation to defend it. Avoid assigning a dramatic career, trauma, subculture membership or generic passion solely to justify a visual symbol. Fictional interview answers should follow resolved design decisions and remain consistent, not repair them through improvisation.

When a consequential tattoo, insert or accessory design changes, retire its old master from that role and make/view the new authorized master under the supporting-artifact method below. Preserve unrelated successful subject/scene requirements, and record which inherited details were deliberately reopened. Compare the new graphic, placement, wear and actual scene depiction with the current story and outfit intent. A plausible biography cannot prove visible fidelity, and a visible tattoo cannot prove biography. Keep character coherence and mark transfer separate from cultural authenticity or identity claims.

## Make consequential supporting artifacts concrete

When the user asks to explain or show a character's designed object, or that object carries a central claim, make an inspectable supporting artifact before relying on it in the scene. A fictional backstory and a prompt are not that artifact. Keep incidental background objects lightweight; this method does not require a complete invented archive for every prop.

Decide what the object is for, who made or uses it within the explicitly fictional premise, and why this person carries or displays this particular piece. Resolve only relevant physical and content details: dimensions or relative scale, material, front/back, graphic hierarchy, meaningful copy, fold/cut/wear and the selected fragment. Keep invented context distinct from real provenance. Do not retroactively assign a newly invented design to an ambiguous fragment in a previous image.

Produce and open the actual clean master or artifact view in an appropriate medium. Give required words intentional content and typography instead of pseudo-writing; use graphic structure without text when that meets the brief better. Retain the full design and the particular crop/side used in the scene. A saved file without a successful view is not a visually inspected master; a mockup does not prove a physical object was printed. Show the standalone artifact when requested, so its explanation can be checked beyond the portrait.

Plan the visible transfer before generating the scene. At the actual receiving scale, choose a few identifying features that can survive: silhouette, dominant mark, value blocks, border, layout or a large character. Consider projected size, crop, finger or fabric occlusion, perspective, reflection and case/material glare. Do not enlarge or rearrange the entire scene merely to display every invented detail. A clean close-up or companion artifact can carry exact information that a natural wide view cannot.

Use the real approved master through the host's supported image-input mechanism when visual conditioning is needed and available. A filename or detailed description is not submitted image evidence. Compare the actual output with the master for the required visible features and correctly oriented physical surface. In a reflected scene, trace the surface and mirror arrangement rather than reversing every graphic by default. Existing [mirror selfie](mirror-selfie.md) geometry guidance still applies.

Review artifact existence, scene depiction and actual legibility separately. Words readable in the master are not necessarily readable on a small photographed object. Inspect the native result and intended display size; enlargement cannot recover missing letters. Do not explain unresolved glyphs as verified copy or claim exact transfer because the correct reference was attached. If a required mark, word or shape is wrong, retain that failure, repair through the supported authorized route and inspect again, or disclose the limit. A separate correct master does not excuse a failed required depiction; conversely, a small but coherent fragment can be adequate when the scene does not require readable copy. Keep the central person, clothing, action and space intact rather than accumulating explanatory props.

For feedback that accepts the character or overall outfit but rejects material behavior or the garment finish, use the continuity method in [capture and styling](capture-styling.md). The latest mixed feedback defines a narrow repair: protect the newly accepted regions even if an earlier attempt had reopened them. Choose the appropriate clean current source, fix the evidenced material/construction defect and compare the transition into preserved regions. A cropped reference does not authorize a claim about its unseen finish, and a requested detail repair is not a new casting brief.

For requested character and pose variation, use [model direction](model-direction.md) to distinguish user-chosen fictional identity from individual self-presentation and actual visible pose. Use [capture and styling](capture-styling.md) to choose front, side, back or partially seen views by the task, rather than inheriting a previous mirror portrait. Compare both the new moment and still-protected requirements in the returned image.

If a face/body relationship is rejected, return to the exact current image and the subject-continuity method in [model direction](model-direction.md). Diagnose perceptual casting fit, projection/connection and capture response separately, then repair the necessary relationship rather than independently retouching a face. Keep accepted action, outfit, personal details and scene within scope. Do not reinterpret a physical/perceptual correction as a request to change gender identity or its diversity.

## Art-direct a useful source

Choose a moment rather than a demographic plus a garment. For people, inspect how gaze meets or misses the lens, head angle changes the eyes, mouth/cheeks carry tension, shoulders and hips distribute weight, and hands engage with the setting. Describe only the connected cues that matter. Do not infer personality from appearance or substitute an identity label for direction.

Place the camera in relation to that moment: height, distance, angle and crop affect the same face or pose. Translate a supported relationship into a new scene rather than copying a person or mechanically assembling every observed cue. A relaxed smile, closed mouth, direct gaze or turned head can each produce different attitudes in different contexts. Verify the whole relationship rather than enforcing an expression blacklist.

Compare a face/upper-body view and the receiving crop when expression is central. Inspect both the intended feeling and required object evidence. If a full garment view weakens the intended moment, another crop or supplementary detail can carry the object evidence; do not normalize every image into catalogue display. Conversely, an expressive crop must not hide the only evidence the task needs.

Choose the key-light direction and size, then environmental/fill light. Contact, cast shadow, highlight and reflection should describe compatible lighting. Control depth of field around actual evidence rather than maximizing blur. Inspect texture at its intended scale: fabric gathers at tension, metal highlights follow the surface, transparent objects affect the light behind them.

When crop is uncertain, compare real source crops in the receiving frame before selecting one. Protect required face, hands, garment or product detail. A useful crop can deliberately omit nonessential areas; protection follows meaning, not a universal full-body rule. Where one source cannot serve all ratios, use an authorized alternate view or recompose the receiving layout.

For a set, coordinate camera/subject scale, light family, color relationships and material finish while varying useful context and detail. Coherence is not identical framing. Sharpness and resolution must survive the largest intended use; a polished thumbnail can conceal poor source detail.

Before accepting an outfit revision, apply the coupled outfit-intent method in [capture and styling](capture-styling.md). A palette-only substitution can satisfy color notes while changing the material, silhouette and personal styling the user meant. Recheck that relationship alongside any reopened fictional detail or story instead of polishing independently successful parts into a conflicting whole.

## Review situated styling before committing the master

If the brief asks the image to convey participation in a scene or practice, test clothing, behavior, activity, surroundings and camera relationship together through [source roles](../../design-pack/references/reference-roles.md). Better garments alone may still read as someone posing in purchased clothes. Build a credible captured interaction or moment supported by the permitted references, without claiming that an appearance proves real cultural belonging.

Consider how styling is worn or used, the person's attention and relation to others or the setting, and whether the camera feels observational, collaborative or promotional. These are compositional hypotheses, not authenticity scores. No distressed styling, named culture, gloomy location, demographic category or expression is a required token. Inspect face/body, full scene and receiving crop together before polishing a source that may not carry the intended relationship.

## Produce and preserve

Use the [generation contract](generation-contract.md) for actual supported inputs and native output. Direct the decisive relationships rather than inventorying every surface or dictating an arbitrary pose coordinate. Keep references bounded by role and existing use/recipient permission; public availability alone does not establish upload or redistribution rights.

Choose asset-first when source identity/detail is the uncertainty, composition-first when the whole frame is unresolved, or a coupled study when image and type need to develop together. Retain a clean master once selected, but allow rejection of a technically fine asset that carries the wrong expression. Keep text, controls and frames separable when exactness or reuse matters. A whole-image generation may suit a concept study; it does not promise production-ready exactness.

## Iterate on the relationship, not just the surface

Review whole-scene coherence as well as the separate subject, styling, space, camera and lighting requirements. Individually attractive parts can still form an unrelated collage. A pass on anatomy, texture or reflection cannot close a failed central relationship. Keep concrete failed or untested requirements in rework instead of averaging them into readiness.

When authorized to repeat until acceptable, use each actual-pixel review to choose the next change. Repair a narrow defect when the underlying scene works; reopen the coupled premise when the same mismatch persists. Do not reroll an unchanged prompt or stack every criticism into a longer exclusion list. Preserve failed outputs and a concise change/reason record, then reopen the new result at whole-frame, use and relevant detail scales. Report a genuine evidence, capability or scope blocker rather than fabricating success.

Reviewer readiness is not the user's OK. Deliver a result for user review when its actual task requirements are supported; only the user's response establishes user acceptance. A requested repeat loop is not permission to publish, install, change unrelated settings or invent a test pass. Preserve any explicit frozen-trial boundary and the latest change to scope or authorization.

## Place against inspected pixels

View the actual source before final label/crop placement. Map annotations in the displayed image coordinate system and test crop/reflow; a planned pose is not a verified anchor. Use actual text, scrims and surfaces when checking reading backgrounds. Check the darkest/brightest local regions, not average image color. Working UI needs semantic controls, meaningful alt text or decorative treatment, and usable missing-image/loading behavior.

## Review three scales

At whole view inspect atmosphere, silhouette and composition. At intended use size compare expression and camera/body relationship to the assigned reference, then reading, crop and object association. At source detail inspect anatomy, garment construction, texture, reflections and compression. Correct anatomy and sharp cloth do not establish the requested attitude. Assess illustrative and factual work by their respective contracts.

After a revision compare both changed and protected regions to the original. Resolve a degraded photo from its clean source instead of accepting sharper labels as overall improvement. Verify export dimensions, transparency, profile and compression where supported; disclose unavailable checks. A large raster is not proof of detail.

Deliver actual output, intended crop/use, source/revision identity, rights limits and observed defects. Ordinary work repairs within authority; [first pass](../../design-pack/references/first-pass.md) freezes and reports read-only when explicitly selected. A prompt-only specification is useful but is not a generated or visually approved image.

For background removal, subject isolation or transparent PNG delivery, read [background removal](background-removal.md). Choose within authorized capabilities, preserve original detail and distinguish alpha-only extraction from retouching or approximate transparency.
