# Image production and placement

This is original practical synthesis, not a rendering engine, photography certification or a technique verified by generated trials. Start with the [brief](../templates/image-brief.md) and the actual intended placement. Keep expressive choices contextual; no universal luxury, beige, studio, cinematic or shallow-focus look.

## Choose what the image must do

Preserve an explicitly requested medium. A photograph/reference-based person-photo request cannot silently become an illustration or SVG because generation or references are unavailable. Report that limitation and deliver independent planning as such; do not disguise substitution as completion.

A photograph can show a real object's details, establish a place or create atmosphere. Illustration can simplify a relationship or invent a world transparently. A diagram can explain what a beautiful photograph cannot. A 3D view can clarify form but may imply unverified geometry/material. A generated scene can explore a concept; it cannot establish factual fit, workmanship, product color or provenance.

For a factual catalogue item, start from approved real assets and verified specifications. Do not invent seams, fasteners, logos, fabric texture, proportions, fit or color and present them as product evidence. If a concept scene is necessary, label it illustrative and retain a clear path to verified detail. Separate an image-only mockup from an implementable interface: words embedded in an image are not accessible controls.

## Translate references without inventing access

Inspect the actual image if available. Record observable framing, subject scale, lighting direction, texture, palette relationships and level of stylization. Distinguish observation from a guessed lens, camera setting, location or material. Public availability does not establish ownership, consent, upload rights or reproduction rights. A saved pin may point to someone else's work.

If a board or asset is unavailable, state the gap and use independent planning where possible. Never call a generic sample “based on your references.” Reuse existing authorization for its actual scope; request only a missing decision that blocks the intended use. Keep project reference ledgers private. For real-person references, use authorized general photographic qualities; do not infer sensitive traits or introduce unrequested identity copying. Confirm the permitted use before sending a reference to a provider; viewing it locally and uploading it are different operations.

## Construct a photographic scene

Choose the subject's action and relationship to the environment before adding effects. A believable stance, object contact or fabric fold often contributes more than an adjective such as “photorealistic.” Specify a camera height and view that reveal the necessary evidence. A low view may give presence but hide a garment's upper construction; an overhead view can explain an arrangement but flatten volume. Distance and perspective must agree with proportions. A focal-length suggestion is a creative instruction, not proof that a generator models optics faithfully.

Place a key light, describe its direction and size, then decide how ambient/fill light and the surroundings affect shadows. Check that contact shadows anchor feet, objects and fabric to surfaces; cast shadows, highlights and reflections must describe compatible light sources. Dramatic lighting may intentionally obscure detail in an editorial image, but it cannot hide required catalogue evidence. Depth of field should separate the intended subject while preserving needed details; maximum blur is not maximum realism.

Describe materials through behavior: a woven textile bends and gathers at tension points; brushed metal has directional highlights; glazed ceramic reflects differently from matte clay. Texture scale should agree with object size. Garment sleeves, hems, pockets, closures and seams should connect through pose and occlusion. Avoid arbitrary surface gloss, plastic skin, repeated identical pores, floating accessories and painted-on fabric. Natural variation is useful; adding random dirt or wrinkles is not automatically authentic.

## Compose for the receiving layout

Before production, draw or describe the frame and expected crops. Mark subject anchors (face, hands, garment/product detail), sacrificial edges and regions where interface text may appear. Quiet overlay space means controlled contrast and detail, not merely an empty-looking area in one preview. Allow the composition to remain coherent when text is absent.

For a wide-to-tall transition, test whether a crop can preserve the subject and desired mass. If not, choose an alternate approved image or an in-flow text arrangement rather than hiding detail behind overlays. Do not promise one generated image will fit every aspect ratio. Record intended `object-fit`/focal placement as project decisions, and test the actual crop instead of only the source file. Keep interface labels and buttons out of the generated pixels for a working UI.

Image-set coherence comes from repeated camera height, light family, subject distance, color relationships and finish, with deliberate variation in detail/context. It does not require identical compositions. Check neighboring images together: mismatched temperature, scale or shadow direction can fracture a page even when each photograph is appealing.

## Build a usable instruction

Use this order: **deliverable/purpose → subject/action/setting → framing/camera → light/material → protected regions/crops → color/finish → exclusions → export**. State positive requirements precisely; a long list of vague negatives cannot define a coherent scene. Use attached approved references only where actually available and supported by the host. Record reference use and production settings if returned; do not invent seeds or promise reproducibility.

## Review at image and interface scale

Review the actual artifact, not the prompt's promises. At full view, judge hierarchy, atmosphere, pose and crop; at useful detail size, inspect hands/feet, joints, object connections, garment seams, repeated patterns, texture transitions, reflections, shadow direction and implausible compression artifacts. Do not assert material identity or exact dimensions from appearance alone. A cinematic image can be successful without being documentary; record which standard applies.

Then place it in the real target frame with actual copy, typography, scrims and controls. Inspect the darkest/brightest local backgrounds under text—including gradient/alpha blending and alternate crops. Exercise applicable hover, focus, pressed and touch presentations. A pleasing neutral preview background does not establish overlay contrast. Check image-loading failure, slow loading, fallback size, meaningful alt text (or decorative treatment), and whether any content was available only in pixels. Use [interface-craft.md](../../design-pack/references/interface-craft.md) for bounded fixture work.

Inspect intended export dimensions, compression, transparency and color/profile behavior. Do not upscale artifacts and report new captured detail. Record failures separately: factual mismatch, anatomical/geometry defect, craft weakness, crop/legibility failure, rights uncertainty, untested condition. In ordinary authorized work, revise the specific cause and recheck affected placements. In [first-pass mode](../../design-pack/references/first-pass.md), freeze first and report these findings read-only; no silent regeneration, post-review recrop or asset swap.

Deliver source/output where available, production history, crop map, intended use, rights/identity limits, accessible equivalent and actual review evidence. A prompt-only handoff is useful when clearly labeled; it is never a generated or visually validated result.
