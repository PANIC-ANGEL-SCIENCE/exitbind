# Direct a believable mirror selfie

Use only when a reflected self-portrait or mirror-mediated outfit image fits the task. Read [capture and styling](capture-styling.md) and [model direction](model-direction.md) for framing, lighting, worn relationships and requested life stage. A visible phone alone does not make a photograph a mirror selfie, and a mirror selfie is not the default for other photographic briefs.

## Inspect the actual mirror relationship

Open relevant source pixels at a useful scale. Distinguish a mirror selfie from a front-camera selfie, an external portrait of someone holding a phone, and a photograph that merely contains a mirror. Note whether that distinction is supported or uncertain. A board grid can identify examples, but small thumbnails cannot resolve finger placement, lens position or reflection detail.

Observe the mirror extent or implied plane, phone height/angle, face occlusion, gaze toward screen or reflection, camera-to-mirror and subject-to-mirror relationships, torso turn, hand/arm connection and the portion of the room that shares the frame. Record visible frame-left/frame-right before asserting the person's anatomical handedness. An original may have been cropped or flipped; do not infer its camera mode or editing history from appearance.

Transfer the selected relationships rather than an exact person, room or pose. Face partly covered, an unobscured face, standing, seated, close and full-length views can all be valid when supported. Do not require a visible mirror border, a particular hand, heavy phone occlusion, a tilted frame, a bathroom or deliberate grime as universal mirror-selfie tokens. Retain enough contextual evidence to make the requested capture mode legible in the final crop.

## Establish one coherent scene

Choose the camera/phone, mirror plane, person and room arrangement together. In a typical rear-camera mirror selfie, the lens points toward the mirror, the reflected phone back faces the viewer and the screen faces the holder. Other arrangements need explicit scene logic; do not silently substitute an external camera viewpoint to show the face more clearly. Phone height, wrist angle, torso orientation and gaze should support the chosen operation without requiring a fixed pose.

Trace the phone-holding hand through wrist, elbow and shoulder. Check a plausible grip, thumb/finger overlap, phone thickness and camera placement; do not invent hidden fingers merely to complete a count. Check the free hand separately and avoid duplicated, fused or detached limbs. An occluded finger is not automatically a defect; an impossible visible joint or unsupported phone is.

Check reflection geometry as a scene, not by applying a blanket horizontal flip. Reflected body, clothing asymmetries, phone, room lines and visible light sources must agree with the mirror plane and selected viewpoint. Ordinary planar reflection should not duplicate limbs, add an unrelated person or expose incompatible phone surfaces. Multiple mirrors, curved mirrors, screen previews and real text need explicit task support and their own consistency checks. Do not add brand marks or text just to demonstrate mirroring. Where an exact asymmetric detail is required, identify its physical position and compare its reflected placement rather than guessing anatomical left from image-left.

## Light the person and reflected room together

Place the relevant window, lamp or other light relative to the person and mirror before specifying its effect. Compare the reflected room's brightness with face, hands, garments, contact shadows and highlights. A mirror redirects the view; it does not justify an independently studio-lit face or flatten the room's light differences. Do not double the illumination merely because a source is visible in reflection.

Flash and no-flash are separate supported choices. A flash-like highlight needs a plausible source position, reflection and falloff; flash, starbursts, dirty glass, glare and blown highlights are not mandatory selfie effects. Available daylight, dim ambient or mixed light can fit different references. Preserve important face and outfit evidence without beautifying every shadow or inventing device metadata.

## Keep requested life-stage and identity coherent

When the task specifies an adult life stage, assess face and whole-body presentation together at source detail and receiving size. Keep that target separate from identity, pose, fashion presence and capture texture. If feedback says the face and body read as different life stages, reconsider the combined casting, grooming/makeup, expression, lighting and processing rather than assuming one facial measurement proves age.

Do not use wrinkle removal, beauty smoothing, infantile proportions or a universal round-face recipe as an age correction. A phone covering the face must not conceal an unresolved facial-direction issue. Preserve the user's selected identity and successful styling within the authorized change scope, and disclose any conflict rather than claiming both exact identity preservation and unrestricted recasting. Reference ages remain unknown unless supplied; visual review supports task fit, not exact age verification.

## Verify the actual returned reflection

Inspect full composition, the final receiving crop and phone/hand/face detail. Separately review capture-mode legibility, hand/phone anatomy, reflection geometry, lighting, requested life-stage fit and protected identity/garment details. Look for an external-camera portrait disguised by a phone, a mirrored room that does not correspond to the person, or flattering studio light that overrides the selected source relationship.

Keep any central failed axis in rework even when the others pass. A technically plausible hand does not establish informal camera language; a convincing room does not establish a believable reflection or the requested face/body relationship. Record unresolved occlusion and detail limits honestly. For an authorized narrow repair, return to a clean source where appropriate, preserve successful regions and recheck the whole reflection. Written prompts and structural tests cannot establish these visual results or user acceptance.
