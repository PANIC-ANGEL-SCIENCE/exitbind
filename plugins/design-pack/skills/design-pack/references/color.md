# Generative color decisions

Do not limit the palette to this pack, a repository's examples or an industry lookup. Generate a role-based system from the actual service, audience, environment, identity and information hierarchy. Choose color-area relationships in context using [elements](elements.md); no starter palette is prescribed.

## Diagnose before choosing hues

First locate the scope of the instruction: interface chrome and states, editorial display, photography, factual product color, or the whole artifact. A restricted UI palette does not imply a restricted photographic gamut. Preserve natural source color unless its treatment is actually specified or deliberately selected within the task. A clearly global palette remains global; do not narrow it silently. Reconcile corrections before production rather than carrying an obsolete treatment forward.

Write the service's primary task and emotional requirement separately. Ask internally: Is use frequent or occasional? Is the screen read for hours, scanned under time pressure, explored for inspiration, or used outdoors? What is the cost of confusing a status? How much competing color is already in content? Which brand associations are supported by this client rather than guessed from the industry?

Examples: a finance service does not automatically need blue; a health service does not automatically need green; a creative service does not automatically need saturated chrome. Associations are culturally and contextually dependent hypotheses. Preserve verified identity and accessibility while exploring alternatives.

## Construct rather than retrieve

1. **Allocate attention.** Decide what should dominate: content, primary action, status or atmosphere. Set the neutral-to-expressive balance and the number of simultaneous attention signals.
2. **Select hue families.** Start from identity, subject/material and desired distinction, then compare an alternative family. Give a reason other than a category stereotype. Separate decorative hues from critical status meanings.
3. **Set lightness architecture.** Establish distinguishable canvas, surface, boundary, text and accent layers. Most readability depends on luminance contrast, not simply hue difference. A palette can be colorful yet unreadable when its values collapse together.
4. **Control chroma/saturation.** Choose chroma for the intended field, image, text and state relationships. If testing reveals fatigue or competition, compare adjustments to area, adjacency, lightness, hue, chroma or grouping rather than automatically desaturating. Low chroma is not inherently sophisticated, and high chroma is not inherently accessible. Consider how the surrounding colors alter perceived strength.
5. **Create semantic roles.** Canvas, surface, text primary/secondary, border, primary action and its foreground, selection, focus, status, and categorical data. Design actual foreground/background pairs, not disconnected swatches.
6. **Produce light/dark systems if needed.** Rebuild the lightness hierarchy and check elevated surfaces, imagery and accent perception. Do not invert each channel mechanically. Use explicit role tokens for each mode.
7. **Test and revise.** Measure the exact rendered pairs; inspect optical balance with real images, text, data, disabled/error states and relevant surroundings. Use color-vision simulations as aids, not a substitute for redundant encoding or actual accessibility testing. Check gamut/export and physical proofs where relevant.

Perceptual color spaces such as OKLCH can help reason separately about lightness (`L`), chroma (`C`) and hue angle (`h`). Their values are design controls, not guarantees of equal perceived contrast. Validate the final gamut-mapped RGB result in the actual output. In print/textile/material workflows use the producer's color specification and proofing process; screen values alone do not specify a physical result.

## Conditional starting strategies

These are hypotheses to test, not presets or universal service rules.

| Context and task | Hue / chroma hypothesis | Lightness and attention strategy | What could change it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frequent, data-dense operational tool | Compare identity-led color fields with lower-chroma framing under representative data | Keep reading groups, selection and data categories distinguishable in either treatment | Existing brand, category density, low-vision needs or screen environment |
| Image-led fashion/art discovery | Compare image-led framing with an expressive interface palette using the same photographs | Inspect image relationships and action recognition; stabilize contrast over changing imagery | Image variation, intended identity and the observed clarity of each treatment |
| Trust-sensitive booking or advisory service | Explore warm or cool families according to evidence of tone, not the sector name | Clear primary action, generous readable contrast, calm status treatment and visible recovery | Audience research may prefer warmth, authority, familiarity or vivid accessibility cues |
| Expressive event/cultural identity | Consider a strong hue or contrasting pair tied to the actual program/material | Distinct display, reading and control roles tested in the chosen expression | Poor print gamut, long reading, mixed imagery, language and accessibility constraints |
| Outdoor/mobile wayfinding | Hue distinguishes categories only with shape/labels; select against environment | High legibility and stable contrast under glare; avoid subtle surface distinctions | Physical testing, ambient light, material finish and local signage requirements |

## Worked example A: library wayfinding

Synthetic task: help visitors locate floors and service points on a printed wall map and mobile guide. Compare a light field with dark route labels against a dark field with light labels under the library's actual lighting. Neither is selected by the institution type. Assign floor categories separate from action, focus and emergency roles; pair category color with a number and name. Test the floor key and navigation controls together without assuming they need the same color treatment.

Build a context strip for each candidate: the busiest floor map, a selected destination, an unavailable lift notice, a focused mobile control and a monochrome print. Record each foreground/background pair including selection under focus. If two adjacent floors become indistinguishable, change lightness, labels or pattern before adding saturation. If the mobile choice fails on the wall substrate, specify separate physical values and proof them; do not promise a match from a screen hex code.

A palette is selected only after the relevant context checks, or marked provisional if proofing is unavailable. This scenario specifies a decision process rather than a preferred hue family. Its checks are illustrative, not observed results.

## Worked example B: operational scheduling

Task: locate conflicts and reschedule quickly during repeated daily use. Compare a near-neutral surface system with an identity-led colored system using the same conflicts, selections and data density. Select by observed scanning, state distinction and intended character. Neither system may depend on color alone to distinguish conflicts from selection.

Make two full state matrices: normal, selected, pending, conflict, disabled and focus. If the accent and conflict color compete, change their role allocation, lightness/chroma or redundant labeling before adding more hues. Test rows with zero, one and many conflicts. Do not infer that a blue interface establishes trust or improves speed without user evidence.

## Worked example C: expressive publication/event

Task: establish a distinctive identity while keeping a long program readable in print and mobile. Derive alternative display, reading and action color relationships from the program's imagery or concept. Compare their occupied areas, adjacency, lightness and chroma with actual program content; neither a specific saturation nor a fixed accent count is selected in advance.

Prototype a poster, a dense schedule and a mobile detail page. If the poster succeeds but small schedule text fails, split expressive display roles from functional information roles; do not discard the entire direction or force display colors into body text. Check the actual print process: a vivid screen color may require a different physical specification, and a proof is needed before promising a match.

## Inspect color in the composition

Put each candidate into the same real-content frame, not just a row of swatches. Record the area occupied by expressive color, the actual background regions behind reading, adjacent image colors and the brightest/darkest background behind every label. Check default, selected, focus, unavailable, error and loading states as relevant. For gradients or motion, test the worst region and time; an average background color is insufficient. A static backing surface or relocated text may solve an unstable pair more coherently than changing the entire palette. Repeat under the target theme, gamut and output conditions.

Use a role sheet: **role → value → foreground/background pair → area/location → state → measured result or not tested → visual observation → decision**. Measured contrast and perceived balance answer different questions. Neither establishes audience preference. Keep actual values, source status and rights in the project, outside this reusable method.

## Deliver the reasoning and verification

For each role, record value, color space, intended pairings, light/dark state, reason, source/status and actual contrast observation. Record exceptions and missing tests. Explain alternatives in user language: “warmer and more intimate,” “quieter around dense data,” or “stronger separation from imagery,” then tie that claim to the prototype. Never present measured contrast, audience preference or conversion impact unless actually tested.

## Image-aware art direction and presentation color

When imagery or material dominates, inspect its actual pixels before choosing the surrounding palette. Note the major lightness ranges, chroma, warm/cool temperature relationships, occupied area, highlight/shadow color and material evidence. An average extracted swatch is not the image: a small vivid detail and a broad colored field behave differently. Preserve believable skin, fabric, metal or other material cues unless an authorized illustrative treatment deliberately changes them; do not grade away evidence merely to match a theme.

Write a relational color hypothesis within each relevant medium: dominant field → visual anchor → contrasting support → selected/active signal. Photograph, interface and presentation may connect through contrast, temperature or repeated emphasis without sharing identical hues. Roles may share a hue or use several; their distinction depends on area, adjacency, lightness, chroma and context. Explain what ties the scene together and what deliberately interrupts it. Constrain competing signals when they weaken hierarchy, not by imposing a universal palette count. Warm/soft, vivid, light and dark systems can all be excellent; muted or black treatments are not an automatic upgrade.

Compare candidate relationships using the actual photograph or scene and exact copy. Test whether the selected accent stays identifiable beside the image's strongest local colors and through overview/detail states. Inspect large-area chroma consciously: a swatch that feels lively may dominate a whole board, while a vivid field may be exactly the intended expression. Decide from the composition rather than automatically desaturating it. Watch competing temperatures, muddy adjacent values, disconnected device shadows and inconsistent material grading across views.

At thumbnail size inspect the color masses, first read and continuity. At intended delivery/use size inspect local contrast around real text, controls and selection markers, including image highlights and shadows. Keep emotional/art-direction judgment separate from measured accessible foreground/background pairs. A beautiful global balance cannot certify local UI contrast; a contrast pass cannot certify tasteful color. Record unmeasured image-only text rather than inventing compliance.

For [portfolio presentation](portfolio-presentation.md), coordinate scene, display type, device surfaces and evidence panels without flattening their roles. Multiple independent concepts should change the organizing proposition and its color relationships, not merely swap a theme on the same layout. An optional [presentation brief](../templates/presentation-brief.md) can hold the hypothesis, context comparison and observed limits. No source-specific palette belongs in the reusable pack.
