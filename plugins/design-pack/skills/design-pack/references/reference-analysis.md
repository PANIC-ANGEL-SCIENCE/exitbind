# Evidence before visual direction

Use this route for a new visual direction, a consequential aesthetic decision, or feedback that the result feels generic or wrong. A tiny local edit does not require a new research project. Research is a design input, not a ritual that blocks work the client delegated.

## Build a useful comparison

1. State the task, medium, audience and screen/object state being designed. Separate a campaign, a discovery surface, a transaction, a community contribution and an editorial story even when they share a subject.
2. Inspect a small set of actual examples, usually three to five for a new direction: a close task analogue, a meaningful alternative and a contrasting example. Include a client reference when supplied. Do not infer an entire preference system from one liked app.
3. Record URL or artifact/version, inspection date, viewport or physical scale, state and evidence type. Distinguish rendered inspection, text extraction, author description and inference. A homepage cannot establish a native app's detail interaction. If access fails, disclose the gap and use available evidence without pretending research is complete.
4. Compare the same dimensions: primary task, information order, content density, image provenance/crop, type roles, color roles, spacing, shape, navigation, interaction visibility and copy function. Describe what is present before interpreting its effect.
5. Identify combinations, not isolated guilty elements. Ask how image styling, hierarchy, density, verbal tone and surface treatment reinforce one another. A serif, beige surface, bright color, badge or shadow has no universal meaning. Nationality and demographic labels are not design mechanisms.
6. Write the transfer decision: retain which relationship, adapt it to which constraint, reject which convention and why. Record at least one counterexample that prevents the hypothesis becoming a blanket aesthetic ban.

Use a compact evidence row: **example/state → observed combination → likely effect (hypothesis) → fit to this brief → transfer decision → validation needed**. Do not claim prevalence from a small convenience sample. Do not infer success, conversion or audience preference from a polished screenshot.

Convert the transfer decision into a concrete composition contract with [visual-methods.md](visual-methods.md). For new public gallery/tool observations and their limits, see [sources.md](sources.md).

## Select, then test the combination

Compare different organizing ideas using the same content at actual size. For a discovery interface, one direction may prioritize the contributor and context, another precise object metadata, and another an intentional editorial story. Choose from the actual brief; none is the default for an industry.

Inspect how much space reaches the user's purpose, what imagery or text hides, whether controls explain their destinations, and whether labels describe real structure. Replacing a rejected soft editorial layout with a generic black-and-bold layout is not evidence of improvement. Change the causal combination and test the intended experience.

When feedback rejects a direction, preserve working behavior while reopening the unsupported aesthetic assumptions. Record the client's preference in the project profile only. Public reusable guidance records the method and its limits, not a private taste blacklist.

## Copy must earn its place

For each line, identify its job: identify a person/object, orient, enable an action, explain state or consequence, provide relevant verified detail, or contribute intentional narrative, atmosphere or brand voice within the brief. If it has no job, delete it before shortening or restyling it.

- Avoid interchangeable mood slogans, generic praise and invented lifestyle claims added merely to make a layout feel designed. Do not replace one empty adjective with another.
- Do not fabricate issue numbers, collection counts, exclusivity, creator testimony, product properties or editorial provenance. Sample content must be marked as such, and decorative counters must not impersonate working navigation.
- Prefer concrete, supported content. A garment detail can identify material or fit only when known; a photo control can say what appears; a return control can name the destination. If the fact is missing, omit it or mark the uncertainty instead of inventing it.
- Expressive language remains available for genuine editorial, cultural or brand work. It needs a specific subject, intentional voice and a purpose in the brief. Emotional effect is legitimate; empty filler is not.
- Honor explicit client prohibitions in the project scope. Do not publish their exact wording or apply it to unrelated clients.

**Deletion test:** remove the line. If meaning, action, orientation and the intended narrative/atmosphere all remain intact, the line probably does not belong. **Swap test:** could the line fit almost any unrelated product unchanged? If so, supply specific substance or delete it. These tests guide judgment; they do not ban short labels or intentionally repeated brand language.

## Completion evidence

Record research coverage and gaps, chosen composition and reasons, rejected assumptions, copy audit, actual-scale inspection and a representative task check. Keep directional fit, usability and technical correctness separate. Successful clicks do not establish that the client likes the art direction. A self-review is not independent validation.

## Worked comparison: different fashion surfaces

A small public desktop-web review on 2026-11-04 compared [66girls](https://66girls.co.kr/), [Common Unique](https://common-unique.com/), [FruitsFamily](https://fruitsfamily.com/) and [SSENSE](https://www.ssense.com/en-us). Rendered-page observations below were supplied by a separate reference reviewer; complementary text extraction inspected FruitsFamily listings, [29CM](https://www.29cm.co.kr/) campaigns and [SSENSE fashion stories](https://www.ssense.com/en-gb/editorial/fashion). These are snapshots, not native-app tests, a representative market sample or proof of commercial effectiveness. No proprietary screenshots or assets are bundled.

| Observed relationship | Interpretation to test | Transfer boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Promotional retail examples place campaign imagery and offers alongside browsing | Competing promotions may divert attention from close inspection when transplanted into a detail task | Promotions can serve a campaign; test attention competition in the destination |
| FruitsFamily's observed grid combines diverse seller photography with brand, item and price information | Specific object information and visual variety may support discovery | Its rounded images, hearts, discounts and varied colors counter a ban on those individual elements |
| SSENSE's observed homepage places cultural context beneath images; dated stories name specific subjects | Editorial language can add a real story and a reason to inspect an image | Editorial purpose differs from fast object identification; do not import surface treatment without content purpose |
| 29CM's extracted text pairs campaign statements with brands/offers | Copy can be intentionally promotional | Text extraction does not establish composition, native-app behavior or client preference |

Evaluate purpose, imagery, hierarchy, copy and action together. Challenge repeated lifestyle framing, unsupported status claims and interchangeable mood lines when they consume attention without helping the brief. Neither a country's design nor an entire visual genre is a failure category. A liked reference does not reveal which features a client values until evidence supports that interpretation.
