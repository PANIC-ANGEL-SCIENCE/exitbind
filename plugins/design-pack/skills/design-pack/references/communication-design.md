# Brand, graphic, print, packaging graphics and editorial

## Brand identity

**Discover:** audience, promise, positioning, competitors, existing recognition, applications, languages and constraints. Separate a brand's claimed qualities from evidence. Identify what should be consistent and where expression may vary.

**Explore:** develop concept territories from the actual proposition. Compare wordmark/symbol approaches, typographic voice, image direction and composition on real applications, not just a logo grid. Use the same sample content to make alternatives comparable.

**Build:** specify identity architecture, logo variants, clear-space/minimum-use rules derived from testing, color roles, typography/fallbacks, imagery, icons, voice and application examples. Include monochrome and constrained uses when relevant. Avoid a universal palette or trendy shape vocabulary.

**Validate:** recognition, legibility at intended sizes, distinction in context, language support, contrast and reproduction. Trademark clearance and rights ownership require their own evidence; a design review is not clearance.

**Handoff:** editable vector assets, exports named by application, token/spec sheet, font licensing, usage guidance and change ownership. A brand guide should show how to make new decisions, not only display a moodboard.

## Graphic and print

**Intake:** message, audience, reading distance, finished size, substrate, print process, quantity, folding/binding, distribution, budget and producer requirements. Missing production specifications mean the result remains provisional.

**Concept and layout:** establish one clear information hierarchy; test actual text, images and language. Consider fold order, page sequence, margins, negative space and display conditions. Show a flat proof as well as a contextual mockup.

**Production:** obtain the printer's current specifications for bleed, trim, safe area, color profile/ink setup, overprint, image resolution, spot colors, finishes and PDF/export format. Do not treat a generic bleed or DPI number as suitable for every process. Preserve editable source, linked assets and permitted font handling.

**Validation:** inspect output dimensions, embedded/outlined fonts as required, missing links, raster quality at output size, transparency, separations, fold/trim alignment, reading order and required copy. Get a physical or calibrated proof when color/material matters. An on-screen mockup cannot prove printed color or finish.

**Handoff:** artwork, export/preflight record, material/finish specification, proof status and unresolved production questions. Producer signoff remains separate from aesthetic approval.

## Packaging graphics

Begin with verified dieline and packaging structure, actual product facts and required content from the responsible party. Identify front-of-pack hierarchy, shelf/thumbnail recognition, opening sequence and barcode/label areas. Treat dieline, folds, glue areas and artwork as separate layers where supported.

Prototype assembled orientation and check seams, overlap, small text, panel continuity and product identification. Verify required labeling with the relevant specialist; do not invent claims, safety symbols or compliance marks. A rendered box is not a structural packaging test. Coordinate physical packaging with the product workflow in [physical-design.md](physical-design.md).

## Editorial and publication design

Map the editorial proposition, reader, content types, sequence and reading rhythm. Build a content inventory and hierarchy before page styles. Design a system for headings, body, captions, tables, pull quotes, images, folios and navigation rather than manually styling every page.

Test representative spreads and difficult content: long headings, multi-column text, footnotes, tables, multilingual paragraphs and image captions. Check widows/orphans where relevant, alignment, consistent references, reading order and image provenance. Digital editions need meaningful structure, navigation and accessibility in the final format, not only in the source layout.

Deliver editable layout, styles, asset ledger, final exports, proofreading status and edition/version identifiers. Separate copy approval from design approval, and rerun affected pagination and cross-reference checks after text changes.
