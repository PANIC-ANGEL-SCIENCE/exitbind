# Construct an aesthetic

Use for appearance-led work alongside [visual-methods.md](visual-methods.md) and [typography.md](typography.md). These are independently authored construction methods and hypothetical directions, not reproductions of templates. Beauty, atmosphere, tension, warmth, delight and recognizable brand expression are legitimate outcomes. They need no fabricated conversion benefit or utilitarian explanation for every mark. Accessibility and truthful content remain constraints; aesthetic ambition remains a goal in its own right.

## Couple the decisions

Start with the actual subject and a desired perceptual experience: monumental, intimate, restless, luminous, tactile, precise—or another justified intention. Translate it into relationships, not a shopping list of effects.

1. **Mass and space:** sketch the distribution of large image/type masses and quiet fields in words or blocks. Choose a single anchor, sequence, balanced comparison or field. Set outer edges and a recurring alignment; decide where a deliberate break creates tension. Give repetition a rhythm: dense group, pause, focal moment. Equally loud modules rarely produce this rhythm by themselves.
2. **Type silhouette:** choose broad/narrow, compact/open, high/low stroke contrast and soft/sharp terminals for the main voice. Relate it to reading and metadata roles. A generous title beside small precise labels feels different from evenly sized headings even in the same family. Compose line endings as shapes without changing meaning. Use the actual language early.
3. **Color distribution:** decide which color occupies the field, which forms the main mass, and where a contrasting note appears. Test lightness, chroma and warm/cool relationships across image, type and material together. Identical swatches can feel different when area distribution changes. Large-area saturation must be deliberate, not an accident of using the accent token everywhere. Derive exact roles and accessible pairs through [color.md](color.md).
4. **Form and material:** choose silhouettes, edge treatments and surface behavior. Repeat a curve, cut or seam at useful scales, while allowing controls clear affordances. Decide whether texture is seen close up, felt as a field, or absent. Coordinate light direction, softness, grain scale and depth. Digital paper is an expressive analogy, not proof of physical properties.
5. **Imagery:** art-direct a set, not just individual attractive pictures. Specify subject distance, camera height, lighting direction, background, color temperature, crop anchor and contrast range. Alternate detail and context deliberately. Protect product evidence; do not color-grade away a material or crop out a needed feature. When images are unavailable, choose a typographic or diagrammatic concept instead of letting generic placeholders define the identity.
6. **Finish:** tune transitions between these decisions. Align visible letter shapes and image subjects, not only boxes. Resolve tangencies at crops/rules, uneven label baselines, abrupt color boundaries, inconsistent shadow softness and accidental line fragments. Quiet surrounding details can strengthen one expressive gesture. Grain, gradients or animation do not automatically create finish.

## Several directions on the same content

Synthetic common brief: a bilingual exhibition about everyday objects needs a title, introduction, three objects, material captions and visit information. All directions use those same facts. These are open-ended hypotheses, not a closed menu, industry mapping or client preference. Values are independent illustrative examples, not measured upstream values or defaults; adapt them to language, medium and content.

| Direction and fit | Coupled construction | Tradeoff / when to change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Photographic intimacy**, when surface and making are central | Let one close material photograph dominate; put a compact title in a quiet neighboring field, with generous prose leading and captions aligned to object edges. Use low-chroma colors related to the photograph, darker ink, soft light and restrained seams. A possible wide-frame start is roughly half image and half reading/air. Alternate whole-object and detail views. | Depends on a coherent licensed image set. If crops hide the objects or lighting disagrees, change the set or organizing idea. Warmth does not require beige or a serif. |
| **Typographic poster**, when the title and cultural voice carry identity | Build the main mass from a large, deliberately broken title; contrast display with compact bilingual metadata. Put objects in a subordinate strip or separate page turn. Try a vivid field, contrasting ink, angular crop edges and no texture; the title silhouette becomes the motif. | Long translations change the mass. Recompose each language rather than compressing it into the Latin outline. High energy must leave visit details readable. |
| **Editorial sequence**, when relationships and interpretation matter | Pair medium images with asymmetric text columns; repeat captions but vary image scale across chapters. Use contrasting title/body voices, quiet canvas, recurring folio/rule and one accent for section changes. A proposed 5:3 image/text split can begin exploration; abandon it where content demands. | Sequential pace can hinder comparison. Keep an overview if needed; do not turn every section into an oversized hero. |
| **Instrument / field guide**, when exact comparison is itself a pleasure | Align object views, stabilize baselines, separate measurement labels and leave generous empty margins around dense groups. Compact text and restrained numeric roles meet a precise line grammar; a small vivid signal contrasts with a quiet field. Use actual data only. | Can feel clinical if everything has equal tiny weight. Give the title and one object stronger presence; never invent coordinates or measurements as decoration. |
| **Playful catalogue**, when discovery and character are central | Group objects by a visible shared property; vary scale within stable row rhythm. Pair lively display forms with calm reading text, coordinated color fields and a repeated cutout silhouette. Keep intentional overlaps in illustration space, away from controls and labels. | Variety can dissolve into a sticker collection. Repeat one relationship across type, image and shape; leave quiet groups between peaks. Avoid assumed audience stereotypes. |

Hybridize around a clear shared idea. Select the dominant organization first; a photographic hero above an unrelated instrument panel is not automatically coherent. A working app need not inherit a landing page's dramatic sequencing: carry identity through type, spacing, color and material while preserving task continuity. For print, space, fashion and objects, translate rhythm into page turns, sightlines, silhouettes or seams and use the relevant production reference.

## From proposal to visible craft

Write a construction card: intended feeling; dominant mass and quiet field; type relationship; color-area relationship; image/light rules; form/material motif; deliberate exception; actual-scale test. This makes a direction buildable without requiring a reference image or subscription.

In ordinary work, examine a thumbnail for silhouette and rhythm, then real size for glyphs, image detail, edges and material. Compare alternatives with the same real content, not better photography in only one. Ask whether the intended atmosphere comes through and which formal relationship causes it. Separate preference, unclear hierarchy and failed assets. Keep meaningful visual pleasure even when it does not change task completion. There is no universal taste score.

In explicit one-shot work, comparisons stay in text before one build. Freeze first; inspections become read-only findings under [first-pass.md](first-pass.md). A failed hypothesis is reported, never silently polished into a second first attempt.
