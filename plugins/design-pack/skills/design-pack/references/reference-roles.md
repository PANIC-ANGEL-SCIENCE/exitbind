# Give each source a job

Reading authority, aesthetic relevance, provider submission and public reuse are different things. Use current task instructions and actual inspected material; keep source identities and filled ledgers in the private project.

## Establish the intended use

For selected sources, record **capture → assigned role and receiving medium → observed relationship → allowed transfer → excluded transfer**. A few short entries usually suffice. Distinguish explicit assignments from inferences and apply the user's latest scope before older taste assumptions.

- **Content/subject:** what is shown, including object, wardrobe, expression, pose and context. A demographic description does not determine expression, attitude or identity.
- **Photography:** camera/subject relationship, timing, light, color, texture and framing. These may differ from interface and presentation treatments.
- **Interface:** information, typography, geometry, navigation, controls and states.
- **Material/scene:** light, surface, depth and spatial relationships; appearance is not proof of physical properties.
- **Presentation:** scale, staging, display type, panel rhythm and evidential framing.
- **Continuity, edit or comparison:** preserving a current series, modifying an existing artifact and comparing rejected work are different permissions.

A mixed source may serve several roles only when the brief and pixels support them. Exclude irrelevant layers from production inputs where the authorized workflow supports a focused capture. A correct role label does not guarantee the generator obeys it; inspect the result.

## Read organized sources by role and coverage

When a user divides references into sections, inspect the actual current sections and relevant source pixels rather than relying on an earlier flat grid. A section title suggests a role; it is not evidence that every item fits it. Keep a short private map of section, inspected examples, assigned role, observed relationships, conflicts and missing coverage. Do not claim an entire section was reviewed from a few visible thumbnails, or treat unseen sections as empty.

Use sections to cover the decisions the task needs, not as a mandatory ingredient list. A face reference may guide permitted casting or expression relationships without copying a real identity or inferring age. An isolated garment does not establish a complete outfit; a room photograph does not identify the user's home or provide every portrait-lighting decision. The main unsectioned board may carry camera/body/context relationships absent from isolated details. Inspect relevant context as well as section-specific detail.

Synthesize a coherent scene across roles rather than transplanting one face, one outfit and one room. Resolve incompatible light, scale, season, garment behavior or camera viewpoint by choosing a supported direction, not averaging all references. Record the decisive transfers and exclusions. A fresh source role never grants new provider-submission or redistribution authority. Section names, source identities, private taste and completed plans remain project-local, not reusable defaults.

For a style-specific reference brief, distinguish defining visual relationships from incidental objects and broad category labels. Record the relationships that must survive reinterpretation, allowed departures and an actual output comparison. A coherent overall story or shared palette does not establish fidelity when distinctive form, material, layering or use disappeared. A source title, brand or social label does not substitute for those visible distinctions. Keep this selection task-local, and revisit it when a repair accidentally normalizes the result.

A source's visual subject and framing are part of its role. Inspect the complete image boundary before calling it portrait, outfit-only, wearer view or object-only; a partial interface view does not establish the source crop. An object can be the main content rather than a prop, and a picture printed on it remains object content unless the brief assigns another role. Transfer the relevant scale, arrangement, viewpoint and evidence without automatically adding a person. Keep uncertain provenance, unseen regions and optional interpretation separate from observed content.

When sources are grouped by gendered or social labels, compare observable pose/expression relationships within and across the groups, including overlap and exceptions. Group labels are not verified attributes of the pictured people. Keep user-specified fictional identity separate from observed styling or movement; no section provides a fixed gender-to-pose rule. Report the actual sampled coverage, not a claim about all people or every source in the group.

For a style-context reference, separate observed visual relationships, user-supplied interpretation and inferred associations. The user's intended mood or material can set the current design target; it does not establish facts about a source person's identity or community. Preserve consequential combinations of surface, cut, layering, wear and detail instead of assigning the image only a color-swatch role. Record unknown or cropped regions and authorized original decisions. Context is project-local and does not become a reusable cultural costume or palette preset.

## Constrain the named layer, not the whole artifact

Read a palette, typography or treatment instruction with its actual scope. UI surfaces, controls, editorial display, photography, product evidence and physical material can have different color contracts. A restricted interface palette does not imply desaturating skin, clothes or surroundings. A photographic color direction does not automatically recolor navigation. A clearly global instruction does apply globally; do not narrow it to evade the brief.

When scope is unresolved, preserve existing source color/evidence while exploring reversible surrounding choices. Ask only if the ambiguity materially affects an irreversible or costly action. State the interpretation briefly. Recheck the latest wording after corrections; do not retain a withdrawn constraint as a preference.

## Observe expression as a relationship

For people, inspect more than category and clothing. Connect gaze direction with camera height and head angle; eyelid/mouth tension with facial asymmetry; shoulders/hips with weight support; hands with actual activity; body orientation with distance, crop and captured moment. Describe visible cues before inferring a feeling. A gaze turned away may look absorbed, evasive or playful depending on the rest of the image.

Transfer the few relationships that cause the requested effect, not a compulsory pose inventory or a replica of a reference person. A smile can be candid, performative or restrained; a neutral face can feel alert or empty. Neither smiling nor its absence is a universal style rule. When the source supports conflicting expressions, choose from the intended moment rather than averaging them into a generic face.

## Connect the person to the situation

When a brief asks for a participant rather than a customer wearing a look, inspect the relationship, not a list of symbols. What is the person doing, how is the clothing used or combined, what connects them to this place or other people, and where does the camera stand in relation to that activity? A visually correct garment on a socially disconnected pose can still miss the brief. Describe these as visible narrative cues and hypotheses, not proof of a real person's identity or membership.

Study styling as an enacted choice: proportion, fit, layering, wear, closures, accessories and movement may relate to the activity. Select only relevant cues from permitted evidence. A venue, prop, tattoo, ethnicity, scowl, distressing or logo is not an authenticity shortcut; no such cue is required. Ordinary clothes or a smile can be equally convincing in a coherent scene. Do not invent biographical facts or read private beliefs from appearance.

Test the relationship in the receiving composition before final asset selection. Compare a useful counterfactual: if the garment or a scene-signaling prop disappeared, would the activity, interpersonal relation and camera position still support the intended reading? This is a diagnostic for this brief, not a demand to remove products or include props. If not, rebuild the moment/styling/context together rather than accumulate signifiers on the same weak pose.

## Keep content truth and photographic craft separate

Establish whether the images are expected end-user inputs, editorial work, factual product evidence or illustrative samples. Respect that content reality without inventing a visual-quality ceiling.

Authentic user content can be intentionally posed, stylishly dressed, carefully framed, attractively lit, sharp and expressive. Candid does not mean dull, badly lit or accidentally composed. Conversely, studio polish is not automatically the right content. Infer the actual photographic range from the assigned references, not a universal campaign-versus-camera-roll opposition. Do not add blur, awkwardness, dirt or uneven light merely to signal authenticity.

Preserve context, subject evidence and approved aesthetic choices while improving crop, scale, type and composition around them. When new illustrative imagery is authorized, art-direct the captured moment, viewpoint, silhouette, light and detail at the required quality while keeping the requested use context believable. Judge the expression in the receiving crop before integrating it. Do not present generated samples as real user photos or invent product construction.

## Inspiration is not the benchmark

A wardrobe or subject source guides what appears. A craft benchmark guides how well the result is composed and finished. Adding a subject source does not cancel the benchmark. Reopen benchmark pixels beside the output at comparable whole-frame and detail scales; identify concrete differences in type, mass, crop, scene, sequence and finish.

Transfer the benchmark's mechanism without cloning its identity or assets. Do not claim parity because a motif is recognizable or because a checklist passed. User acceptance remains unconfirmed until actually received.

## Verify access and transfer

Inspect the current relevant source and record missing sections. A historical capture is not current verification. Distinguish an empty source from denial or unreadable content; do not invent its contents. Resolve essential gaps while continuing independent work. Use alternatives only when the task permits them.

For generation, map actual approved image inputs under the [generation contract](../../mockup-image/references/generation-contract.md). A source name or local path in prompt prose is not image conditioning. Keep only relevant inputs, preserve use/recipient boundaries and report unsupported conditioning honestly.

At review, check both the positive transfer and unintended leakage: did subject style become app chrome, presentation perspective become distorted UI, or a previous output become the new geometry by default? Ordinary work repairs within scope and retains originals. Frozen trials preserve defects. No source ledger proves perception, rights or aesthetic quality by itself.
