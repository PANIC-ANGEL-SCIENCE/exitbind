# Author the portfolio as a visual argument

A presentation is a visual argument around the product. Choose hero board, explanatory sequence or combined output; use [screen production](screen-production.md) for its actual UI. Keep project identities and completed [presentation briefs](../templates/presentation-brief.md) outside this pack.

## Choose the communication genre separately from the file format

A request may use “portfolio” for the delivery container while asking the work to behave like a product-introduction website, editorial feature, exhibition or sales presentation. Resolve that reading behavior from the latest brief before defaulting to process captions and device mockups. For a product-introduction concept, lead with what the product offers and how its distinctive operation becomes visible; decide how one section invites the next through image, copy, evidence and relevant controls. Do not invent customer results, endorsements, prices or working destinations to fill a familiar landing-page recipe.

A pair of stills or PDF can depict consecutive website sections without becoming an implemented website. Distinguish presentation copy and proposed calls to action from controls inside the embedded product. Give each an intelligible purpose and truthful sample state. Review the intended scroll or section transition as well as the delivered page turn; do not claim responsive behavior, clickable controls or deployment from static art. Keep the selected genre local to this brief rather than turning all portfolios into landing pages.

## Read a sequence as a sequence

View the supplied source sections at useful scale and in order. A cover supports cover observations only. Separate the presentation from the embedded product: image/type/device masses, scale changes, cropping, depth, density, type roles and transitions. Identify what each view adds. Labels or charts in a reference are not verified research for a new project.

Use [reference analysis](reference-analysis.md) to turn an observed mechanism into a construction, and [source roles](reference-roles.md) to preserve current restrictions. A source for subject matter does not replace the craft benchmark. Compare both at whole-frame and readable-detail scales during review.

## Commit to the delivery sequence before page polish

If the requested deliverable is a connected sequence, treat it as one composition with multiple viewing moments. Decide the actual page order, each page's new evidence, the transition and what should remain in memory at the end. Build an actual-content strip or spread together, not a finished first page followed by a supplementary layout assembled from leftovers. This planning order is scoped to connected presentation work; a single board or unrelated pair does not need a narrative invented for it.

Choose rhythm from the content: shifts in image/type scale, density, viewpoint, readable product evidence, pause or sustained intensity. A page turn can reveal, contradict or deepen the opening. These are options, not a mandatory hero → process → outcome template, fixed page count, alternating light/dark rule or demand for unequal panels. The next page must earn its place, including when deliberate repetition enables comparison.

Compose thumbnails with the selected imagery or representative crops, real title and actual interface states. Check both the sequence silhouette and the useful information at reading size before commissioning final assets that would lock the layout. If a needed camera distance, pose or image extension is absent, revisit the image/layout relationship early instead of filling a leftover column with decoration. Keep maker-selected page geometry revisable while user-fixed requirements survive.

## Build the argument in thumbnails

Write the sequence argument and each board's contribution, then make small actual-content compositions together before expensive finals when the relationship is uncertain. Vary the organizing relationship: image-to-type scale, object/context pairing, depth or crop, state comparison, or spatial rhythm. Do not merely rotate the same phones. Use comparable content and fidelity; no fixed count, grid or silhouette is required.

Judge the thumbnails before adding microcopy and effects. What is the first read, what is revealed, and what evidence rewards a closer view? A large headline plus repeated hardware can be appropriate, but must earn its place through the subject. If the first read is merely the template structure while the subject's expression disappears, recompose. Presentation should expose a convincing product, not conceal an unresolved one.

Let identity recur in the product’s content framing, type relationships or meaningful controls, not only in a logo and device wrapper. Combine contextual imagery, readable screen views and useful detail where they carry different evidence. A sequence needs development, not uniform density. Alternate or sustain intensity according to its content. A close-up can reveal a detail, a full view can restore context, and a state comparison can explain an operation. Repetition works when it makes a meaningful difference perceptible. Avoid giving every panel identical framing merely for consistency.

## Develop the product and its presentation

Choose the order that exposes the largest risk. An uncertain interaction benefits from a state strip; an uncertain hero needs an actual-content composition; an uncertain subject/crop relationship may need a coupled image and layout study. Do not finish every screen before testing whether the overall argument works, or freeze an expensive image simply because it came first.

Resolve final screen states at natural viewport proportions with exact text and coherent object identity before final device placement. Keep clean image masters, screen source/exports, scene, display type and annotations separately editable when precision or reuse matters. Separation protects fidelity; it does not prohibit simultaneous creative development.

Set the board's field and dominant masses. Position product views with consistent scale or credible perspective, then relate display type and supporting evidence to them. Inspect visible baselines, device edges, crop tangencies, ground/contact shadows and the direction/softness of light. A perfectly cut-out phone with incompatible lighting still reads as pasted in. Exact UI text should not depend on repeated whole-board generation when a reliable compositing route exists.

Choose a board frame and delivery size separately from screen size. Do not stretch screens to fill space. Decide which crops are atmospheric and which are evidence; provide an unobscured readable view if the claim cannot otherwise be checked. Caption, display and real UI copy have distinct roles. Keep truthful sample/concept labels without turning the board into a process report.

## Matched series: reuse sources, vary the argument

Resolve concept count separately from image count using the latest request. Two images can be independent concepts or connected boards of one concept. Record shared anchors: object identity, exact copy, type roles, color/material/light language and state logic; record what each board changes.

For exact continuity, reuse the same clean content masters and editable screen sources across boards. A flattened hero board is a useful style/continuity reference, not a high-quality replacement for its underlying photo or UI. Do not repeatedly extract and regenerate source content through successive boards. This also permits states to be developed before either final board, rather than locking the second to an accidental first layout.

If a generator-only route is appropriate, inspect the first board and pass it through the authorized image-input mechanism as a disclosed continuity reference when useful, alongside required source assets. Follow the [generation contract](../../mockup-image/references/generation-contract.md). Preserve every native original and compare identity/detail in both. Do not claim exact continuity from a text-only description, or independent generation after using an earlier output.

For new independent concepts, comparison-only prior outputs stay out of generation/edit inputs. Their presence in a workspace does not make them authorized templates. Never attribute owner acceptance to a proposed direction.

## Export and reopen the actual sequence

When a PDF is requested, assemble the ordered pages into that PDF, then render and inspect its actual pages and a full-sequence overview. For a two-page PDF, inspect both the side-by-side spread and the page-one-to-page-two turn; neither isolated PNGs nor a collage in the source editor proves the delivered reading experience. For other sequence formats, inspect their actual navigation/order instead. Do not impose PDF or facing-page binding on a brief that did not request it.

Check page order/count/dimensions, cropping, font/glyph survival, image detail and readable UI at the intended viewing size. Across pages compare selected object identity, naming/language, type roles, control silhouettes, action/state logic and any deliberate exceptions. A repeated photo can create continuity; it should not erase the promised development. Reopen all affected pages and the overview after changing a shared source, export or order. Review the final bytes, not an earlier attractive preview.

A successful export or radius calculation is only a technical check. The full sequence still needs a visible judgment of hierarchy, transition, subject attitude and whether the presentation reaches the commissioned ambition. Keep a failure in rework, including when every individual page is technically consistent. The optional [outcome review](../templates/outcome-review.json) records scoped gates when useful; it cannot judge aesthetics or certify that a view occurred.

## Review the work, not the recipe

Open the whole series together for hierarchy and pacing, each board at delivery size for reading, and original-size crops for image/type/material fidelity. Compare against actual benchmark pixels, not only the previous revision. Examine whether expression, type, image mass and the product's distinctive operation reinforce one another. A useful interaction can still read as an instructional worksheet when its presentation only enumerates steps; determine whether that meets this brief. Equal frames, strong display type and restrained staging remain valid when they carry the intended argument.

Decide separately whether required content/states are present and whether the portfolio's central craft ambition is met. If the latter fails, keep the direction in rework; do not call it a nonblocker merely because the files are readable and consistent. Repair the relevant source layer, or replace a designer-selected organizing idea when local refinements leave its failure intact. Reopen the integrated result and protected regions. A completed storyboard, high reference count or passing archive test is not aesthetic success.

Preserve accessible product behavior, factual fidelity, rights and private-source boundaries. Static screens do not prove live interaction. Ordinary production iterates; explicit frozen trials follow [first pass](first-pass.md) and report defects without disguising a later revision as first.
