# Industrial/product, spatial/environmental and fashion design

Use this pack for discovery, concept development, specification, prototype planning and critique. Production-ready claims require the relevant specialist tools, materials, measurements, testing and authorized professional review. Never infer physical safety or code compliance from an attractive image.

## Industrial and physical product design

**Intake:** intended user/task, context of use, dimensions, loads, environment, interfaces, manufacturing process, volume, cost, lifespan, maintenance and end-of-life. Separate measured requirements from estimates. Include ergonomics and access needs.

**Explore:** compare mechanisms and form concepts against user tasks before rendering finishes. Produce annotated sketches, rough models and interaction/assembly sequences. State tradeoffs among usability, size, material, manufacturing and repair.

**Develop:** define functional architecture, part hierarchy, material/process rationale, dimensional interfaces, tolerances to be established by engineering, assembly/disassembly, finish and service access. Use a bill-of-materials outline and unresolved-risk list. Do not invent engineering tolerances.

**Prototype:** progress from form/fit mockup to functional prototype according to uncertainty. Test grip, reach, force, wear, balance, assembly, misuse and environmental conditions where appropriate, using safe authorized methods and relevant specialists. Keep simulated performance distinct from physical test results.

**Handoff and acceptance:** drawings/CAD when available, units, revisions, interfaces, materials, process assumptions, test plan/results, manufacturing questions and required engineering signoff. A rendering or text specification is a concept handoff until physical constraints are verified.

## Spatial, interior, exhibition and environmental design

**Intake:** verified site dimensions, users, program, movement, sightlines, lighting, acoustics, climate, installation constraints, accessibility, budget and maintenance. Treat supplied plans as versioned evidence; mark unmeasured areas.

**Explore:** map arrival, orientation, movement, pause, use and exit. Compare spatial organization and experience before decorative treatments. Study human scale, visibility and sensory load. Wayfinding must work for people unfamiliar with the space and cannot depend only on color or one language.

**Develop:** plan zones, circulation, furniture/fixtures, lighting intent, material palette, signage hierarchy, acoustic intent and operational responsibility. Coordinate physical and digital touchpoints where relevant. Distinguish concept layouts from construction documents.

**Prototype:** use scale plans, models, walk-throughs and full-size critical details as appropriate. Test viewing distance, reach, movement and signage comprehension in realistic conditions. Digital walk-throughs do not verify construction, accessibility clearances or acoustics.

**Handoff and acceptance:** dimensioned documents at supported precision, material/specification schedule, fixture/signage details, installation and maintenance intent, evidence gaps and required architect/engineer/local-code review. Never certify egress, structural loads or public safety from this pack.

## Fashion, textile and wearable design

**Intake:** wearer, occasion/function, climate, body/fit range, movement, identity, garment category, material availability, production volume, cost, care and lifespan. A style preference is not a fit measurement.

**Explore:** build silhouettes and material/color stories anchored in the brief. Compare proportion, layering, texture, construction and movement. References may inform construction or mood without copying protected prints or brand identity. Include cultural context and intended meaning when it affects interpretation.

**Develop:** create flats and detail views, construction intent, closures, seams, fabric/trim specifications, measurement points, fit sample assumptions, grading requirements, labeling/care questions and bill of materials. State which pattern and fit details are unverified. Do not infer fabric drape or shrinkage solely from a photograph.

**Prototype:** toile/sample, material swatches and movement/fitting sessions as appropriate. Check comfort, mobility, opacity, skin contact, closure access, laundering behavior and consistency across intended sizes using relevant testing and expertise. A virtual try-on is not a fit approval.

**Handoff and acceptance:** tech-pack content, pattern/spec versions if produced, sample comments, material provenance, care/labeling requirements from responsible specialists, production tolerances and outstanding fit tests. Do not call a moodboard or generated garment image manufacturing-ready.

## Cross-disciplinary coordination

Maintain one shared concept and separate measurable contracts for each medium. For example, wearable branding, packaging, launch film and storefront may share expressive language while needing different color reproduction, dimensions, accessibility and proof methods. Assign an owner and required evidence to every interface between disciplines.

Evaluate material efficiency, repair, reuse and disposal as design tradeoffs when relevant. Do not make environmental benefit claims without a defined basis and evidence.
